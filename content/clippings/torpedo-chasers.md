![Five Torpedo Chasers for Government. Clipping, 1917.](/media/archive/clippings/1917-defoe-news-clipping.jpg)

Five torpedo chasers, built for the government by the DeFoe Boat & Motor Co., of Bay City, were on cars and ready for shipment to “somewhere” on the Atlantic coast this morning. These craft represented $60,000 in cold cash.

Although the company received the contract from the navy department last October, it was not generally known that the plant was engaged on work for the government, few even in this city having any knowledge of the fact. The company went into competition with several other boat building concerns and walked away with the contract without any trouble. Under existing circumstances, however, it was not considered wise to make any announcement of the competitive achievement.

The torpedo chasers are constructed of the very best obtainable materials. They are 40 feet long, 10 feet beam, with four feet draught. The frames are of full length stock, no splicing being allowed. White oak was used and the planking is Port Orford cedar, brought here from Oregon. The navy department is taking the entire output of this material from the source because of its durability. All the fastenings are copper riveted. The castings are solid bronze of special government composition. The bow stem is cased in brass castings for the purpose of rigidity and durability. These craft are equipped with the Winton gasoline engines, built by the Winton Engine Co., of Cleveland, Ohio, with 85 horse power. The engines are of the heavy duty type and weigh in the neighborhood of three tons each. While the weight is excessive the government needed the boats also for towing purposes when exigencies might arise. They are constructed to stand any kind of weather and almost any kind of hard usage.

This design of craft is wanted at torpedo proving grounds and practice stations where torpedoes are tested out and also for the benefit of gunners at naval training stations. A similar contract was secured at the same time by the Herschoff Co., of Newport, R. I.

The loading of the craft on cars in front of the Defoe Boat & Motor Co. yesterday afternoon attracted considerable attention, each requiring an entire flat on account of its size.
